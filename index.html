Myntra Insider Loyalty Revamp

From "collect points, maybe use them someday" to "shop a little more often and feel the benefit right away".

1

Overview

The whole case in one screen. Everything below expands on these five points.

Problem

Rewards feel far away and hard to understand, so most members never move beyond the entry tier.

Idea

A simple 3-tier ladder, a visible progress bar, and perks you get immediately.

Who it helps

Shoppers who buy 2 to 4 times a year: the "almost loyal" group and the biggest one to convert.

Expected result

About +10% repeat orders among members in 6 months, with cost per order flat.

The ask

Approve a two-phase pilot, starting with 5% of members and a control group.

Note on numbers: every target, threshold and duration in this case study is a planning assumption. They would be validated against internal data before any commitment.

2

The problem

Loyalty only pays off if members come back, and today's program makes the reason to come back too faint. Fashion e-commerce has high returns and heavy discounting, so a repeat order from an existing customer is far cheaper than winning a new one.

Unclear value

Members cannot say in one sentence what each tier gives them.

Slow progress

The next tier takes so long to reach that it feels unreachable.

Points feel like fine print

Rewards are earned at checkout but are hard to see, trust and spend.

Same offers for everyone

A first-time buyer and a monthly buyer see similar perks.

Discounts do the loyalty job

Big sale events drive orders, so loyalty adds little on top.

These are hypotheses to confirm with data and member interviews, not proven findings.

The proposed solution

Four changes: count orders instead of rupees, give benefits at once, show progress everywhere, and make points easy to spend.

Today (assumed)After the revamp
Tier rulePoints or spend thresholds that are hard to trackNumber of orders in the last 12 months
BenefitsMostly points and occasional offersImmediate perks at every tier: free delivery, early sale access
ProgressHidden in the profile pageProgress bar on home, cart and order confirmation: "1 more order to Gold"
Using pointsMany steps, unclear valueOne tap at checkout, rupee value shown up front
Personal touchSame for everyoneNudges and perks matched to the segment

Proposed tier ladder (working names and numbers, to be tested)

Silver

Join free
  • Base points on every order
  • Welcome perk on first order

Gold

3 orders in 12 months
  • Free delivery
  • 12-hour early sale access
  • 1.5x points

Platinum

8 orders in 12 months
  • Priority support
  • Free return pickup
  • 24-hour early sale access
  • 2x points

Why orders and not spend? A count of orders is easy to understand, hard to game with one big purchase, and rewards the behaviour we want: coming back.

6

User journey

Riya is an "almost loyal" shopper who has ordered twice this year. Here is how the revamp changes her next three months.

  1. Home pageShe sees a bar: "1 more order to unlock Gold: free delivery and early sale access".
  2. CartThe bar reappears, with the points she will earn on this order and their rupee value.
  3. Order placedThe confirmation says "You are now Gold", and perks apply from her very next order.
  4. Two weeks laterA 12-hour early access alert arrives for a sale on brands she has bought before.
  5. Next checkoutShe pays part of the bill with points in one tap, with no code to copy.
  6. Month threeAt 6 orders, a nudge shows she is 2 orders from Platinum, with perks in plain words.

Rollout plan

The pilot is the decision needed now: a 5% test with a held-out control group, scaled only if repeat orders rise and cost per order stays flat.

Weeks 1 to 8

1. Pilot

  • 5% of members get the revamp
  • 5% held out as control
  • Measure repeat rate and cost
Gate ARepeat rate up vs control, cost per order flat
Weeks 9 to 16

2. Expand

  • 25%, then 50% of members
  • Tune tier numbers and perks
  • Add nudges per segment
Gate BReturns and cost within the set cap
Week 17 onward

3. Full launch

  • All members
  • Current status kept 12 months
  • Weekly metric review

How the pilot test works

Groups

5% of members see the revamp, and a matching 5% keep the old program.

Length

At least 8 weeks, with repeat orders read again at 90 days.

Split by segment

Results are cut by occasional, almost loyal and power shoppers.

Decision rule

Scale only if repeat rate rises and every guardrail metric holds.

Risks and mitigations

The biggest risk is paying for perks that members would have bought anyway.

RiskWhat could happenMitigation
Cost creepFree delivery and extra points raise cost per orderCost cap per member, tracked as a guardrail
CannibalisationPerks go to orders members would have made anywayCompare against a holdout group that sees no change
AbuseMany tiny orders to reach a tierMinimum order value counts toward progress
Returns riseMore orders also bring more returnsTie perks to kept orders; watch return rate
Transition confusionMembers feel they lost points or statusKeep status for 12 months and show a clear "what changed" page

Open questions and next steps

Five facts are missing, and each one changes a number in this case study.

Open questions

  • What are today's tier rules, points value and baseline repeat rate?
  • What is the budget cap for perks per member per year?
  • Can free delivery be funded by smaller sale-event discounts?
  • Which teams own the home, cart and checkout screens?
  • How does Finance treat unspent points as a liability?

Next steps

  1. Pull baseline data for the three segments.
  2. Interview about 10 members from each segment on the perks they value.
  3. Design the progress bar and the one-tap points flow.
  4. Take the pilot plan and cost cap to leadership for approval.
